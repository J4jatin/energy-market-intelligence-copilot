EDF — Électricité de France — Competitor Intelligence Brief
(Illustrative sample document for the Market Intelligence Copilot knowledge base)

Company overview
EDF is a French state-owned multinational utility and one of the world's largest electricity producers,
with a generation fleet dominated by nuclear power. It operates across France, the UK, Italy, and
other markets, and was fully renationalised by the French state in 2023.

Strategy and positioning
EDF's strategy centres on nuclear as low-carbon baseload: extending the life of its existing reactors,
building new EPR reactors in France and the UK (Hinkley Point C, Sizewell C), and complementing nuclear
with growing renewables and grid/retail services.

Nuclear and renewables
EDF's competitive identity is nuclear leadership. It also expands solar, onshore and offshore wind
(via EDF Renewables), hydro, and storage, and invests in EV charging and energy services.

UK and European presence
Through EDF Energy (UK) it is a major supplier and nuclear operator. It competes in retail and
generation across several European markets.

Key competitive takeaways
EDF's low-carbon nuclear base differentiates it from the wind-led strategies of RWE, Ørsted, and
Vattenfall. Its main challenges are the cost and schedule risk of new nuclear builds, reactor
availability, and high debt — balanced by stable, dispatchable low-carbon output.
