EU Emissions Trading System & Carbon Pricing — Market Note
(Illustrative sample document for the Market Intelligence Copilot knowledge base)

What it is
The EU Emissions Trading System (EU ETS) is the bloc's cap-and-trade carbon market. It puts a price on
CO2 emissions from power generation and industry, tightening the cap over time to drive decarbonisation.

Impact on power markets
Carbon prices feed directly into the marginal cost of fossil generation. Higher EU ETS prices make coal
and gas plants more expensive to run, improving the relative economics of renewables and nuclear and
influencing wholesale power prices.

Policy evolution
Under "Fit for 55", the cap tightens faster, free allowances phase down, and a second emissions trading
system (ETS2) extends carbon pricing to buildings and road transport. A Carbon Border Adjustment
Mechanism (CBAM) prices embedded carbon in certain imports.

Competitive relevance
Generators with high fossil exposure (Uniper, parts of RWE) are more sensitive to carbon costs, while
renewables- and nuclear-heavy players (Ørsted, EDF, Iberdrola) benefit relatively. Carbon-price
volatility is a key driver of trading and hedging strategies.

Takeaway
The EU ETS is a structural force pushing capital toward low-carbon generation and shaping the
profitability of every European energy competitor.
