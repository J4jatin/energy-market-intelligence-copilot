German Offshore Wind Auctions & Capacity Outlook — 2025
(Illustrative sample document for the Market Intelligence Copilot knowledge base)

Overview
Germany continues to expand offshore wind in the North Sea and Baltic Sea, with dedicated auction
rounds and expansion targets that step up capacity through 2030 and 2035. Auctions allocate seabed
sites and grid-connection slots, and have introduced dynamic-bidding and negative-bidding components.

Key developers
The main developers competing for German offshore sites include RWE, EnBW, Ørsted, Vattenfall, and
consortia involving international energy majors. Winning bids depend on turbine supply agreements,
grid-connection timing, and cost of capital.

Grid connection
Offshore grid connections are built by transmission operators; delays in offshore converter platforms
and north-south corridors (see SuedLink) are a recurring bottleneck that affects when farms can deliver
power to southern demand centres.

Cost pressures
Supply-chain inflation, higher interest rates, and turbine availability have pressured offshore
economics, causing some developers (notably Ørsted) to reassess projects. Larger turbines and
standardised platforms are the industry's response.

Takeaway
Offshore wind remains central to Germany's energy transition, but execution — auctions, supply chains,
and grid timing — determines which competitors capture the growth.
