Green Hydrogen in Germany — Strategy & Market Note
(Illustrative sample document for the Market Intelligence Copilot knowledge base)

Policy context
Green hydrogen is central to Germany's National Hydrogen Strategy, targeting significant electrolyser
capacity and hydrogen imports to decarbonise hard-to-abate sectors: steel, chemicals, heavy transport,
and long-duration power backup.

Industry activity
Energy companies including RWE, Uniper, EnBW, and Iberdrola are developing electrolysis projects and
hydrogen-ready gas plants. Uniper leverages its gas storage and LNG import infrastructure for future
hydrogen; RWE pairs hydrogen with its renewables and flexible generation.

Infrastructure
A planned hydrogen "core network" of pipelines would connect production, import points, and industrial
demand clusters. Import corridors (via pipelines and shipped derivatives like ammonia) complement
domestic electrolysis.

Challenges
Green hydrogen remains costly versus grey hydrogen; scaling depends on cheap renewable power,
electrolyser cost reductions, offtake certainty, and infrastructure build-out.

Takeaway
Hydrogen is a strategic growth vector for most large German energy players, but near-term economics
and infrastructure timing keep it a long-game bet rather than an immediate earnings driver.
