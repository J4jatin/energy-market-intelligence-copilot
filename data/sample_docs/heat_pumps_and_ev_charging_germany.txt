Electrification of Heat & Transport in Germany — Market Note
(Illustrative sample document for the Market Intelligence Copilot knowledge base)

Heat pumps
Germany is pushing heat-pump adoption to decarbonise home heating, supported by building-energy rules
and subsidies. Heat pumps shift heating demand onto the electricity system, growing retail power demand
and creating new customer-solutions markets. E.ON and others expand heat-pump installation and service
businesses.

EV charging
Electric-vehicle adoption drives demand for public and private charging. EnBW operates one of Germany's
largest public high-power charging (HPC) networks; E.ON expands public and depot charging. Fast-charging
along motorways and urban hubs is a competitive battleground.

Grid implications
Electrifying heat and transport raises and reshapes electricity demand, increasing the value of smart
tariffs, demand flexibility, and distribution-grid upgrades. Time-of-use pricing (e.g., Octopus-style
smart tariffs) helps shift EV and heat-pump load to low-price, high-renewable periods.

Competitive relevance
Retail- and network-led players (E.ON, EnBW) benefit from electrification through service growth and
grid investment, while tech-led retailers (Octopus) compete on flexibility software.

Takeaway
Electrification of heat and transport is a major long-run demand driver and a growth arena for
customer-solutions and charging businesses across the German market.
