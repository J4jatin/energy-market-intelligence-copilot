SuedLink and German Grid Expansion — Infrastructure Note
(Illustrative sample document for the Market Intelligence Copilot knowledge base)

The north-south challenge
Germany generates large volumes of wind power in the north but has major industrial demand in the
south (Bavaria, Baden-Württemberg). Moving that power requires high-voltage direct current (HVDC)
transmission corridors.

SuedLink
SuedLink is one of the largest HVDC underground-cable corridors, designed to carry northern wind power
to southern load centres. It is co-developed by transmission system operators including TransnetBW
(part of EnBW) and TenneT. Additional corridors such as SuedOstLink complement it.

Why it matters
Grid expansion is the critical enabler of the Energiewende: without corridors, northern wind is curtailed
and southern demand relies on other sources. Permitting, local opposition, and construction timelines
make grid build-out a persistent bottleneck.

Competitive relevance
Transmission operators (TransnetBW/EnBW, TenneT, Amprion, 50Hertz) run these regulated projects.
Delays affect offshore/onshore wind developers (RWE, Ørsted, Vattenfall) whose output depends on
connection and transmission capacity.

Takeaway
Grid corridors like SuedLink are as decisive for the transition as new generation — timing of these
projects shapes power flows, prices, and curtailment across Germany.
